1. The Physics of Negative Pressure Ventilation

In a modern enclosed poultry house, fans do not blow air onto the birds; instead, high-capacity exhaust cone fans extract air from one end of the building, creating a slight vacuum (negative static pressure). Atmospheric pressure outside the shed then forces fresh air to surge through carefully calibrated inlet louvers or evaporative cooling pad tunnels.

When static pressure is maintained precisely (typically between 20 and 28 Pascals, depending on shed width), the incoming air enters with sufficient momentum to hug the ceiling apex. Here, it mixes with the warm air generated by heaters and bird body heat before gently descending into the bird breathing zone without chilling the litter.

The Rule of Thumb CFM Calculation:

Total Air Volume (CFM) = Bird Count × Max Bird Weight (lbs) × 1.8 CFM/lb. For a 40,000-bird flock at 6.0 lbs, total peak CFM needed = 40,000 × 6.0 × 1.8 = 432,000 CFM. Dividing by 44,000 CFM per 50" fan results in 10 exhaust cone fans.

2. Calculating Target Air Speed & Wind Chill Effect

During extreme summer heat (>35°C), high air velocity across bird feathers produces a cooling effect equivalent to a 5°C to 8°C temperature drop. However, this wind chill requires a minimum air velocity of 2.5 meters per second (500 feet per minute) uniformly distributed across the house cross-section.

If dead air pockets or low-velocity zones occur along the sidewalls, heavy birds in those areas will suffer severe heat prostration and respiratory alkalosis. AviTech's 50-inch butterfly shutter cone fans utilize precision aerodynamic fiberglass diffusers to maintain laminar flow with zero back-draft turbulence.

3. Static Pressure Settings Across House Widths

Shed width dictates the required static pressure. The wider the building, the higher the pressure required to project air to the center:

  • 12-meter shed width: 18 – 22 Pa static pressure
  • 15-meter shed width: 22 – 26 Pa static pressure
  • 18-meter shed width: 28 – 35 Pa static pressure

4. Evaporative Cooling Pad Operational Timing

A common mistake made by farm operators is activating evaporative cellulose cooling pads too early in the morning when ambient relative humidity is still above 75%. Adding moisture to already saturated air creates a humid sauna that stifles the birds' panting mechanism.

Always ensure that cooling pumps only engage when ambient temperatures exceed 28°C AND shed relative humidity is below 70%. In our automated climate controllers, this interlock is programmed by default to safeguard bird health.
